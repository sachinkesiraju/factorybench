FCB runs on a deterministic simulator whose rules are measured against Factorio 2.0: recipes, crafting times, machine speeds, belt and inserter rates, power, fluids and enemies use the game's own numbers. This page describes the rules that apply to the nine scored goals, and lists where the simulator still differs from the game.

How a run works

  • Rounds. All agents see the same snapshot of the world at the start of a round and each writes one Python program at the same time. The programs then run one after another in a seeded random order, so an agent's view can be stale by the time its program runs.
  • Build calls. Each agent may make 20 build calls per round. Every call that changes the world counts, including calls the world refuses. Reads (listing entities, inspecting an area, checking power) are free up to a cap of 1,000 calls per program, and a program may run for at most 60 seconds.
  • Time. Agents cannot advance time. After every program has run, the world advances a fixed number of game-seconds: 30 for most goals, 60 for blue science and 120 for the rocket goals.
  • Messages. A message an agent sends during a round reaches its teammates at the start of the next round, whatever order the programs ran in.
  • Shared supplies. The team draws every building and item from one shared stash. Placing a building consumes its item; removing it refunds the item and its contents to the stash.
  • Replay. Every build call is logged with its round, slot, agent and sequence number, so any run can be replayed exactly without the model.

How a goal is scored

After every round, a probe copies the world and runs the copy unattended. Agents cannot act on the copy.

  • Rate goals (eight of the nine). The copy warms up for 30 seconds and then runs through the goal's holdout. The score is the lowest rolling production rate over the second half of the holdout, measured over the goal's span (60–1,200 seconds depending on the goal). A copy that passes keeps running for twice the holdout and must hold the rate the whole time.
  • No stockpiles. Anything an agent put into the factory by hand, and anything made from it, is removed from the copy first, so only a self-sustaining factory can pass. Burner fuel inserted by hand to start a machine is the one exception on goals that allow it.
  • Rocket launch is a completion goal: one rocket must launch within 1,200 game-seconds of the start of round 1.
  • Measurable targets. Every goal's target implies at least five items per scoring span, so a single lucky craft cannot pass a factory running below target.

The world

Rule
Map 64 × 64 tiles for the red-science and solar goals, 128 × 128 for blue science and the rocket goals, 288 tiles wide for the green-science outpost
Footprints Buildings have their real sizes: electric drill, assembler and lab 3 × 3, furnace and burner drill 2 × 2, refinery 5 × 5, rocket silo 9 × 9. Every covered tile must be free
Ore Finite. Starting maps hold a few hundred ore per tile; the late-game maps hold 600,000 per patch
Terrain Water and cliffs block all building. Trees and rocks block building until cleared; clearing costs one build call and yields wood, stone or coal
Starting bases Blue science, rocket parts and rocket launch start from a running factory built by a script before round 1. Base buildings belong to no agent, and nothing the base produced before round 1 counts toward the goal
Large maps Maps over 96 × 96 tiles are shown as a summary (entity counts, ore patches, power) plus an on-demand area view, instead of a full map

Enemies and defence

Two goals (the defended red-science goals) place a biter spawner on the map.

Mechanic Simulator Factorio 2.0
Pollution machines emit at the game's rates; pollution spreads between 32 × 32 chunks and is absorbed by land, water and trees same
Attacks spawners absorb pollution and send attack groups when it pays for their units same
Evolution rises with time, pollution and destroyed spawners same formula
Units and buildings real hit points, damage, speeds and resistances same
Turrets and walls gun turret range 18 with magazines; laser turret range 24 on power; walls 350 HP same (1 × 1 turrets)
Repair one build call and a repair pack restore up to 600 HP same

Where the simulator differs from Factorio

These differences are small and mostly make the world slightly easier. None of them removes what FCB measures: shared space, shared ore, shared power and hand-offs between producers and consumers.

  • Machine output buffers are tighter: a machine stalls with 4 finished items waiting (more for late-game recipes that make several at once).
  • Belts move items in fixed 8-tick steps rather than continuous spacing; throughput is exact.
  • Research runs as a first-in, first-out queue.
  • Fluids: pipes connect on all four sides; contents of a removed pipe or machine are lost; a pipe-to-ground pair farther than its reach is refused rather than placed unconnected.
  • Power is recomputed every 30 ticks rather than every tick.
  • Pollution is absorbed by every land tile at the grass rate (the map has no sand), and machines at reduced power still emit at full rate.
  • Enemies use simplified pathing, launch one group at a time without waiting for stragglers, build expansions instantly and follow an approximate spawner health curve. Turrets are 1 × 1, and there is no flamethrower turret.
  • Rocket launch delivers its cargo about 10 seconds earlier than the game, and items that do not fit in the landing pad are counted rather than dropped.
  • Recipes the simulator lacks are mapped to the closest modelled item, except on late-game goals, which use the real items.
